PhD Student in Design · Zhejiang University

Yue Chen 陈悦

I am interested in how people and machines live together. My current research looks at functional emotional states inside large language models and how they quietly shape model behaviour, and asks how this technical phenomenon can become a human–AI interaction problem that design can observe, explain and intervene in.

I studied design and art & technology at the China Academy of Art, worked on product and interaction design projects with Huawei, the Zhejiang University Binjiang Institute and Geely Design, and took part in exhibition research at the China Design Museum. My work has been shown in art museums and at a visualization conference. Earlier papers cover social interaction for blind and low-vision people, generative industrial design and human–machine co-creation, published at CHI, in the Journal of Engineering Design and elsewhere. In my spare time I rebuild old machines from the history of Chinese writing and information technology as playable web pages.

01Research

What models say and what they do: emotion activation in LLMs and human–AI trust

Users usually judge whether an AI agent is reliable only from the answer it finally gives. Preliminary evidence suggests that emotion-related activations inside a model may already have changed its actions while its output shows no corresponding emotional expression. From what a model says alone, users cannot fully judge its current state or the basis of its actions, and their trust may become miscalibrated.

Using methods from explainable AI, the research examines the relationship between internal emotion activation, linguistic expression and actual action. It does not try to decide whether models have emotional experiences like ours.

Interaction design for states we cannot see

Follow-up work asks how an interface can present model states that cannot be observed directly, how to explain the relationship between emotional expression and task behaviour, and when users can keep delegating to a model and when they should adjust the task or take over. The range of emotion regulation, the grounds for explanation, ways of undoing actions and the conditions for human takeover are all treated as part of the design's safety boundaries.

Planned research contexts include GUI intent inference, drone control and autonomous driving.

03Exhibitions & AI Art

The same question asked another way: how technology changes the way we look.

The Kuleshov Experiment

Interactive installation · April 2026 · China Design Museum

A visitor stands in front of the camera; the installation records their face and cuts it together with film shots into a short montage, which they can download by scanning a QR code. The work takes its cue from Lev Kuleshov's editing experiment: the same expressionless face, followed by different shots, is read as showing different emotions.

04Design History & Museums

The relationship between people and machines has a history of its own. I am interested in modern design education, networks of people in architectural history, and how a Chinese design discourse took shape.

A permanent exhibition in preparation at the China Design Museum, setting the Bauhaus and VKhUTEMAS alongside modern Chinese design education. I research the I. M. Pei thread and coordinate the concepts for two interactive installations.

05Retro Remakes

My interest in the history of technology, made hands-on: old objects that are discontinued, hard to repair or survive only as drawings, rebuilt as web pages you can play with, with a focus on where Chinese characters met machines. Sources are patents, classical texts and public-domain materials wherever possible.
